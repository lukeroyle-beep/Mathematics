## 1. Decimal reduction and the complete sum class

For odd `L>=5` and `3<=j<L`, the decimal word is
`[L-j-1 sevens] 5 [j-1 sevens] 5`. With `R_L=(10^L-1)/9`,

```
n=7R_L-2-2*10^j,
9n=F(L,j)=7*10^L-18*10^j-25,
S=7L-4, P=25*7^(L-2).
```

Consequently the full sum condition is `9S | F`, and the full witness
condition is exactly `225S*7^(L-2) | F`. The latter equivalence does not
require coprimality. Since `S=3 mod7`, depth is `v_7(F)=v_7(n)` and the
witness needs depth at least `L-2` at this same length.

Suppose `gcd(S,10)=1` and one actual position i passes the full sum.
For a second position j, write `q=|j-i|`, `m=min(i,j)`. For `q>0`,

```
F(L,j)-F(L,i)= +/-18*10^m*(10^q-1).
9S | F(L,j)  iff 9S | 18*10^m*(10^q-1)
             iff  S |  2*10^m*(10^q-1)
             iff  S | 10^q-1
             iff  d | q,     d=ord_S(10).
```

The division by nine is exact integer divisibility on both sides; S is
unchanged. Then `2*10^m` is a unit modulo the odd integer S. In particular
when `v_3(S)=a`, the original difference needs `a+2` factors of three;
18 contributes two and leaves **all a** required in `10^q-1`. Cancelling
18 directly modulo S would lose this precision and would be invalid.

The equality case j=i is immediate. Therefore, including both parities,
the full set of compatible actual positions is exactly

```
j=i+hd,
h_min=ceil((3-i)/d)=-floor((i-3)/d),
h_max=floor((L-1-i)/d),
h_min<=h<=h_max, h integer.                       (1)
```

No assertion that the least nonnegative representative is an actual
position is needed. In the present coprime case `25 | F` for every
actual j, so the full sum condition also implies `225S | F`.

## 2. Joint spacing and the role of an exact-depth position

Modulo seven, 10 has order six; `10^6=8 mod49`. Binomial lifting gives
`ord_(7^k)(10)=T_k=6*7^(k-1)` for every `k>=1`.
Two positions of depth at least k therefore have difference divisible
by `T_k`: subtract their F values and cancel the units 18 and `10^m`
modulo `7^k`. If they also pass the full sum, their difference is a
multiple of

```
Q_k=lcm(d,T_k).                                   (2)
```

Conversely, from one such position, every actual shift by `Q_k` satisfies
both conditions, so this is the complete joint class whenever nonempty.

If `Q_k>L` and an **actual** position has exact depth k, no second actual
position can have depth at least k: actual positions differ by at most
`L-4`. A deeper position would be such a second position. Hence the
maximum over all full-sum positions is exactly k. For `L>k+2`, this
excludes the entire length from the full witness test. The sharper
sufficient gap is `Q_k>L-4`; the requested `Q_k>L` is ample.

Without the known actual position, a large period proves only at most
one, not that there are none. Also `Q_2>L` cannot alone compare a
depth-one position with a deeper one: the former is not in the depth-two
class. Section 5 supplies the needed extra argument.

## 3. Rechecking the fixed-lift density construction

We read [the merged proof](../fixed-depth/PROOF.md) and both appendix
positions in [SUBMISSION.txt](../fixed-depth/SUBMISSION.txt). The following
reconstruction specifies why the proof works for **any fixed compatible
exact-depth lift**, especially `e=9 mod42` when k=1.

Fix k. Put `M=48*7^k`, `H=7M`. Choose positive A,B such that
`3^A=1 mod(H/3)` and `17^B=1 mod H`; for example
`A=12*7^k`, `B=6*7^k` work by the unit orders modulo 16, 3 and
`7^(k+1)`. On independent indices m,t>=0 set

```
a=12+Am, b=1+Bt, S=3^a*17^b, L=(S+4)/7,
S0=9034497, L0=1290643, U=3^A, V=17^B.
```

Then `S=S0 mod H`, `L=L0 mod M`, L is odd, `L=3 mod16`, and
`L=16 mod27`. The three position conditions are:

* At three, `q=(7*10^L-25)/9` is an integer, and
  `3^(a+2)|F` iff `10^i=q/2 mod3^a`. Division by 2 is modular.
  From `10^16=145 mod243` and `7*145-43=4*243`, the target is
  `1 mod27`. The subgroup generated by 10 modulo `3^a` is exactly
  the residues `1 mod9`, with order `3^(a-2)`. Thus the exponent
  is one class modulo `3^(a-2)`, congruent to 0 modulo 3.
* At seventeen, `10^8=-1 mod17` and `10^16=1+8*17 mod289`,
  so the order is `16*17^(b-1)` and 10 generates the units.
  At the frozen length the target `(7*10^L-25)/18` is
  `5=10^7 mod17`, hence one exponent class, `7 mod16`.
* At seven, the target `(7*10^L-25)/18` is a unit. There is one
  exponent class modulo `T_k`, always `3 mod6`. Exactly one of
  its seven lifts modulo `T_(k+1)` has depth at least k+1; each
  of the other six has exact depth k. Freeze **one** such lift e.
  The frozen length residue makes it work on the entire grid.

The first two exponent periods are coprime; their overlaps with the
third are 3 and 2. Every chosen e is 0 modulo 3 and odd, so CRT gives
a unique class with least representative r and period

```
W=16*7^k*3^(a-2)*17^(b-1).
```

Increasing either index gives `r'=r mod W`: `L'-L` is divisible by
S and by M, so its powers of ten agree modulo `3^(a+2)` and `17^b`,
and the chosen e remains fixed. The extra two powers of three are
retained because `ord_(3^(a+2))(10)=3^a` divides `L'-L`.
For the circle points `x_(m,t)=r/W` this gives

```
U*x_(m+1,t)=x_(m,t), V*x_(m,t+1)=x_(m,t) in R/Z.
```

Let E be the intersection of the closures of the joint tails
`m,t>=N`. Compactness makes E nonempty and closed. Its points are
exactly limits with both indices tending to infinity. Shifting an
approximating sequence down proves forward invariance; shifting it up
and taking a convergent subsequence supplies preimages. Thus
`U(E)=E=V(E)`.

If E were finite, the U action would be a permutation, so a positive
integer D would clear every denominator in E. For
`0<epsilon<min(1/2,1/(U+1))`, compactness gives **uniform** proximity
`||Dx_(m,t)||<epsilon` throughout a whole tail quadrant. Otherwise
a violating joint-tail sequence would limit to a point of E and
contradict its denominator. Now fix a row t=N. Its signed errors z_m
satisfy `U*z_(m+1)-z_m` integral of absolute value less than one.
Therefore `W*z_m=C` is a fixed integer, possibly zero or negative,
and `D*r=C mod3^(a-2)` along the row as a tends to infinity.

With `X=10^L`, the two fixed integer polynomials

```
P(X)=X^7-10^4,
Q(X)=10^max(-C,0)*(7X-25)^D-18^D*10^max(C,0)
```

vanish modulo unbounded powers `3^a`: use the order modulo `3^a`
for `10^(Dr)=10^C`, the sum congruence for `7X-25=18*10^r`,
and `7L=S+4` for `X^7=10^4`. If P,Q were coprime over Q,
a cleared polynomial Bezout identity would express a fixed nonzero
integer as their integer-polynomial combination. Unbounded powers of
three cannot divide it. So P,Q share a complex root, even though the
modular roots vary.

P is irreducible: for its positive root `z=10^(4/7)`, the element
`z^2/10=10^(1/7)` has degree seven by Eisenstein at 2 for `X^7-10`.
Hence P divides Q. At a primitive seventh root of unity zeta,

```
|7*z*zeta-25|^2-|7*z-25|^2=350*z*(1-Re(zeta))>0,
```

which prevents both roots from giving the same absolute value in
`(7X-25)^D=18^D*10^C`. Contradiction: E is infinite.

The published closed-set theorem of Furstenberg applies to the
multiplicatively independent integers U and V: a closed infinite
circle set invariant under both maps is the whole circle. We freshly
checked the statement at Bourgain, Lindenstrauss, Michel and Venkatesh,
*Some effective results for x a x b*, ETDS 29 (2009), section 1.1,
printed p.1705, [publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/01225FAD40EEBC38F3AE1A5C119D0267/S0143385708000898a.pdf/some-effective-results-for-ab.pdf).
The proof supplies even surjectivity; no measure or entropy claim is
being substituted. Every joint tail is dense since its closure contains E.

For fixed k,

```
W/L=(112*7^k/153)*S/(S+4) -> lambda_k=112*7^k/153>1.
```

Given any `t0 in I`, choose fractions `r/W` approaching
`t0/lambda_k` in an ordinary arc inside (0,1), with both indices
tending to infinity. Then `r/L -> t0`; eventually `i=r` is an
actual position with `i/L in I`. Quantitatively, when
`|r/W-t0/lambda_k|<delta`, the error in `r/L` is at most
`lambda_k*delta+4*t0/(S+4)`. First choose a small delta and then a
late tail. This works even near either endpoint because t0 has a
positive interior margin. L is unbounded, giving infinitely many
distinct lengths, and imposing `L>k+2` discards only finitely many.

No step in the density argument used a special numerical value of e.
It only used fixedness, CRT compatibility and exact depth. This checks
the specific lift below, rather than inferring density of one lift from
density of a union of lifts.

## 4. Maximum depth for each fixed k>=2

On this grid,

```
d=lcm(3^(a-2),16*17^(b-1))=16S/153,
6 | d, 7 does not divide d,
Q_k=7^(k-1)*d,
7d/L=784S/(153(S+4))>1.                            (3)
```

The last inequality is `631S>612`. Therefore the actual depth-k
position furnished above is the unique sum-compatible position of depth
at least two. The maximum over **all** compatible positions is k.
Any other compatible position has exact depth one: its displacement
is a multiple of d, hence six, so it passes modulo seven, but cannot
pass modulo 49. This proves the claimed infinite family for every
fixed `k>=2`, including attainment in each prescribed I.

## 7. Scope of the strengthened result

For **every fixed k>=1 and every nonempty open I inside (0,1)** there
are infinitely many distinct odd lengths `L>k+2` whose maximum depth
over all full-sum-compatible actual positions is exactly k, attained
at an i with `i/L in I`. These whole lengths fail the full witness
condition. Their construction is an existence proof, not an evaluated
consecutive interval or an effective bound for the first length in I.

Keep four results distinct:

1. Consecutive exclusion: all lengths **1 through 10,000,000 inclusive**.
2. Earlier isolated exclusion: `L=(898129*3^193+4)/7`, maximum depth six.
3. Two new isolated exclusions: the appendix lengths here, maxima four
   and seven. Their equal 14,997 count in the p example does not identify
   it with the earlier isolated length.
4. The infinite fixed-maximum-depth family proved above.

The last three do not extend the consecutive interval. The all-length
`{5,7}` problem remains open: every remaining admissible L and j must
still be uniformly ruled out from `225(7L-4)*7^(L-2) | F(L,j)`, or a
witness exhibited. Fixing k before allowing L to grow is not a proof
at the diagonal `k=L-2`. The existing qualitative sublinear valuation
bound has no effective starting length that closes the remaining finite
obligation. Other admissible digit sets also remain, so the full A398769
conjecture that every term contains digit 1 remains unresolved.
