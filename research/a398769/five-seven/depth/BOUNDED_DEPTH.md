# Growing depth: bounded representatives and the unresolved quantifier

These are elementary ordinary proofs, not Lean formalizations. Keep the
merged [Route B](../effective/ROUTE_B.md), the proportional exclusions,
and the complete certificate through 10,000,000. The new prime filters
and the family in [FAMILY.md](FAMILY.md) do not remove those hypotheses.

## An exact criterion covering all residual lengths

This criterion is not restricted to `S=c*3^a`. Let `L>=5` be odd,
`S=7L-4`, and `5` not divide `S`. Define

```
R_L=(10^L-1)/9,
u=(7*R_L-2)*2^(-1) mod S,
d=ord_S(10).
```

These operations are valid since `gcd(S,10)=1` and `S` is odd. From
`n=7R_L-2-2*10^i`, the full digit-sum condition `9S | F` is exactly
`10^i=u mod S`. If `u` is outside the subgroup generated by 10, there
are no positions. Otherwise obtain its unique exponent `s mod d`.
This subgroup test, order and discrete logarithm can be found by finite
enumeration for each specified length; no efficient factorization or
uniform running-time claim is needed for the criterion.

For each `k>=1`, the order of 10 modulo `7^k` is
`T_k=6*7^(k-1)`. Indeed its order modulo 7 is six and
`v_7(10^6-1)=1`; the binomial expansion lifts the order by seven at each
step. Thus there is a unique `r_k mod T_k` with

```
18*10^r_k = 7*10^L-25 (mod 7^k).
```

The right side is a unit modulo 7. Compute the generalized CRT for
`s mod d` and `r_k mod T_k`. It fails precisely when
`s != r_k mod gcd(d,T_k)`. Otherwise let `rho_k` be its **least
nonnegative** representative and let `W_k=lcm(d,T_k)`.

For the strict target interval set the integer endpoints

```
A=floor(41L/100)+1,
B=ceil(42L/100)-1 = floor((42L-1)/100).
```

The first compatible position at or above `A` is exactly

```
j_k = A + ((rho_k-A) mod W_k),
C_k = max(0, 1+floor((B-j_k)/W_k)).                 (1)
```

Here a remainder modulo `W_k` belongs to `[0,W_k)`. Therefore the
simultaneous full digit-sum / `7^k` condition has a position in the
interval **if and only if `j_k<=B`**, and (1) counts all such positions.
This includes strict endpoints and cases where the least representative
is larger than the actual length. For the broader position domain use
`A=3, B=L-1`; additional inherited restrictions may then reject positions.
At `k>=2` the modulo-49 position table already enforces the inherited
`i=3 mod 6` condition. Neither table nor criterion asserts full divisibility
unless the power is raised to `k=L-2` and all remaining restrictions hold.

This gives an effective valuation upper bound whenever `C_k=0`: every
sum-compatible position in that interval has `v_7(F)<=k-1`. It applies to
**every** residual length passing the 5 filter, including all lengths
outside the special constructed family. It is an exact decision criterion,
not an evaluated uniform bound on those lengths.

## What growth of the modulus actually proves

If `W_k>B-A`, there is at most one position counted by (1). Already
`T_k>B-A` suffices. The compatible residue at the next depth is a lift
of the old one, and the sets of bounded positions are nested. Thus after
uniqueness is reached, either that position survives or it disappears;
another position cannot replace it. The representative need not be of
size comparable to `W_k`. Consequently `W_k>L` by itself does **not**
imply `j_k>B`. A synthetic exact example is a common residue 5 modulo
101 and 103: the period is 10403, yet the position 5 is below 10.

For each fixed length, `F>0` when `L>i>=3`, so each valuation is finite.
The elementary upper size bound `F<7*10^L<7^(2L)` (for `L>=2`) ensures
that enumeration of depth eventually stops by `k=2L`. This merely proves
termination for a specified length. Its coefficient exceeds the needed
`L-2`. Even using the full known divisor gives only

```
7^v <= F/(225S) < 7*10^L/(225S)
```

for a sum-compatible residual pair: `25 | F`, `9S | F`, and
`gcd(25,9S)=1`, while 7 divides none of `225S`. The logarithm of this
upper bound is `L*log(10)/log(7)-log(L)/log(7)+O(1)`; its leading
coefficient is greater than one. This size argument cannot supply the
required uniform strict bound below `L-2`.

## Fixed powers versus the growing target

The supplied infinite family rigorously attains depth at least four,
while passing the **full** digit sum, inside the entire open ratio gap.
Its supplied control has valuation exactly four. For a generalization
`S=c*3^a`, [FAMILY.md](FAMILY.md) proves the precise overlap and spacing
conditions involving `ord_c(10)`, `k`, and the actual length. One may not
vary `c` merely to increase the length: doing so also changes the target
modulo `c`, its subgroup membership, the exponent class and its order.

For fixed `c=898129`, the sufficient spacing argument multiplies the
spacing-to-length ratio by seven at each additional depth beyond four.
It therefore does not prove existence for every fixed `k`. Failure of
that sufficient inequality does not prove nonexistence either: (1), not
the spacing alone, decides a specified length.

**First unproved step for the intermediate question:** for every `k>=4`,
produce some length `L>10^7` for which subgroup membership and CRT
compatibility hold **and** `j_k<=B`, with the varying parameters proved
to satisfy those conditions. No such uniform construction, and no
uniform constant upper bound on attainable depth in the interval, is
proved here. Additional individual depths are finite partial results.

**First unproved step for a regional exclusion:** prove `C_(L-2)=0`
(or rejection by another necessary condition) uniformly over every
remaining length in this interval. The effective computation of (1) for
each input does not prove this universal statement. The inherited uniform
`v_7(F)=o(L)` still has no effective starting length. A global proof must
also cover the other five remaining ratio regions listed in
[the merged coverage note](../proportional/COVERAGE.md).

## Scope of computation

`checks_crt.py` checks (1) and the exponent reductions against independent
decimal construction at every odd length 5 through 301 that passes the
5 filter, every position `3<=i<L`, and depths 1 through 4. It checks both
the complete position interval and the strict ratio interval. Separate
controls cover incompatible noncoprime CRT, compatible residues whose
least representative is too large, and strict endpoint exclusion. These
are reduction checks, not new all-length coverage.

`checks_depth_samples.py` separately exhausts all full-sum positions
`3<=i<L` at `L=(898129*3^193+4)/7`. To prove completeness, it checks
that `d=56133` is the exact order of 10 modulo `c`: `10^d=1`, while
its powers at `d/3,d/7,d/11` are respectively `66723,631165,615263`,
none equal to one modulo `c`. The full sum positions therefore form
exactly one class modulo `lcm(d,3^191)=77*3^191`.

The checker enumerates all 14,997 representatives between 3 and `L-1`,
including both parities. Modular powers give nonzero residues modulo
`7^10` for every representative, hence exact valuations, with maximum
six. It independently concatenates the four decimal blocks at **every**
position to check `n mod S=0` and the same valuation modulo `7^7`.
There are exactly 150 sum positions inside the target ratio interval;
their maximum is four. All other positions fail the full sum by the
complete exponent-class argument. Thus this excludes the **entire one
length** for the original subclass, using the inherited residual reduction.
It does not exclude all lengths in the ratio interval or all members of
the infinite family. The full receipt records the class, endpoints,
histogram and maximal positions. Consecutive complete coverage remains
1 through 10,000,000 inclusive.
