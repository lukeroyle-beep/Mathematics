# Independent audit: arbitrary fixed seven-adic depth

25 November 2026. **Mathematical verdict: verified as written.**
The following reconstructs the argument, including the potentially fragile
steps. No mathematical repair or changed hypothesis is required. The
separate numerical verdict and reproduction commands are in [README.md](README.md).
This is an ordinary mathematical proof using a published topological theorem,
not a Lean formalization.

## Statement and decimal reduction

For every fixed integer `k>=1` and every nonempty open interval `I` contained
in `(0,1)`, there are infinitely many distinct odd lengths `L` with an integer
`3<=i<L` such that

```
i/L in I,
225(7L-4) | F(L,i),
v_7(F(L,i))=k,
F(L,i)=7*10^L-18*10^i-25.
```

Here the decimal word, read left to right, is

```
[L-i-1 sevens] 5 [i-1 sevens] 5.
```

An empty first block is allowed. Numbering from the units place by zero,
start with `R_L=1+10+...+10^(L-1)` and change the sevens at positions `0,i`
to fives. Then

```
n=7R_L-2-2*10^i,
9n=7*10^L-18*10^i-25=F,
S=7L-4,
P=25*7^(L-2).
```

Consequently `PS | n` is equivalent to `225*S*7^(L-2) | F`. The full sum
condition is **`9S | F`**, not merely `S | F`: no cancellation of nine
against a factor of S is legitimate. In the proposed family
`S=3^a17^b`, this requires `3^(a+2) | F`.

For actual positions `i>=3`, `25 | F`. Since `gcd(25,9S)=1` in this family,
`9S | F` implies `225S | F`, equivalently `25S | n`. Also `S=7L-4` is
coprime to 7. Thus, after the non-7 factors pass, a full witness requires
**`v_7(F)>=L-2` at this same length L**. Exact fixed k does not supply that
growing threshold.

## 1. A grid whose length residues are frozen

Fix k once and for all. Write `T_d=6*7^(d-1)` and

```
M=lcm(16,T_(k+1))=48*7^k,
H=7M=48*7^(k+1),
H/3=16*7^(k+1).
```

The integers 3 and 17 are coprime to `H/3` and H respectively. Choose
positive A,B with `3^A=1 mod(H/3)` and `17^B=1 mod H`. Existence follows
from the finite unit groups. These constants depend on k, not the indices.
For independently varying `m,j>=0`, put

```
a=12+Am, b=1+Bj,
S=3^a17^b, L=(S+4)/7,
U=3^A, V=17^B.
```

The seed is `S0=9034497`, `L0=1290643`. We have `S=S0 mod(H/3)` and both
vanish modulo 3. As `gcd(3,H/3)=1`, `S=S0 mod H`. In particular L is an
integer and `L=L0 mod M`. Since S is odd, L is odd. Direct seed reduction
gives `L0=3 mod16`; also `7L=S+4` and `a>=12` give `L=16 mod27`.

## 2. The three position congruences

### The factor of three, with the extra nine retained

The elementary lifting identity for positive h is
`v_3(10^h-1)=2+v_3(h)`. Hence the order of 10 modulo `3^a` is `3^(a-2)`
for `a>=2`. Its subgroup is exactly the residues congruent to 1 modulo 9:
containment follows from `10=1 mod9`, and both groups have `3^(a-2)` elements.

Let `q=(7*10^L-25)/9` in the integers and let `u=q*2^(-1) mod3^a`.
The integer division is valid because `7*10^L-25=-18 mod9`. Now

```
F=9(q-2*10^i),
3^(a+2) | F  iff  10^i=u mod3^a.
```

The order modulo 243 is 27, and `10^16=145 mod243`. Since
`7*145-43=4*243`, the frozen `L=16 mod27` gives `q=2 mod27`, so `u=1 mod27`.
Thus u belongs to the required subgroup, and its unique exponent class
modulo `3^(a-2)` is 0 modulo 3, since `ord_27(10)=3`.

### The factor of seventeen

Here `10^8=-1 mod17`, so the order modulo 17 is 16. Moreover
`10^16=137=1+8*17 mod289`; its difference from one has 17-adic valuation
exactly one. The same elementary prime-power lifting argument gives
`ord_(17^b)(10)=16*17^(b-1)`. This is the full unit-group size, so every
unit is generated by 10.

At the frozen `L=3 mod16`, `10^L=14 mod17`. The target
`(7*10^L-25)*18^(-1) mod17^b` reduces to `5=10^7 mod17`, since
`7*14-18*5-25=-17`. It is a unit and has exactly one exponent class
modulo `16*17^(b-1)`. That class is 7 modulo 16, hence odd.

### Exactly k factors of seven, including k=1

Modulo 7 the successive powers of 10 are `3,2,6,4,5,1`; its order is six.
Also `10^6=8 mod49`, so `v_7(10^6-1)=1`. Therefore
`ord_(7^d)(10)=T_d`, the full unit-group size.

The target `(7*10^L-25)*18^(-1)` is `-1 mod7`. Thus it has a unique
exponent class modulo T_k; every representative is 3 modulo 6. Its seven
lifts modulo `T_(k+1)` give all unit lifts of that target, exactly one of
which has depth at least k+1. Choose any of the other six and call it e.
It gives valuation exactly k. Because L is frozen modulo `T_(k+1)`, the
target modulo `7^(k+1)` is constant on the entire grid, so the same e works
at every grid point. This reasoning includes k=1.

For a direct k=1 check at the seed:

| Lift i modulo 42 | 3 | 9 | 15 | 21 | 27 | 33 | 39 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| F modulo 49 | 28 | 7 | 35 | 14 | 42 | 21 | 0 |

Thus the submitted e=3 is an exact-depth-one lift.

### Generalized CRT and its full period

Let `p3=3^(a-2)`, `p17=16*17^(b-1)`, `p7=T_(k+1)=6*7^k`.
Their pairwise gcds are respectively `1,3,2`. The first and third
exponent residues agree modulo 3; the second and third are both odd.
These are all compatibility conditions. There is a unique simultaneous
class, with least nonnegative representative r and period

```
W=lcm(p3,p17,p7)=16*7^k*3^(a-2)*17^(b-1).
```

These congruences give `9S | F(L,r)` and exact depth k. This is initially
only a congruence assertion: r need not yet be less than L.

## 3. Compatibility and the joint-tail circle set

For one increase of either grid index, use primes for the higher point.
Then S divides `S'-S`, and `L'-L=(S'-S)/7` is an integer. Since `gcd(S,7)=1`,
S divides `L'-L`. The frozen length congruence also makes the difference
divisible by 16 and `T_(k+1)`.

The order modulo `3^(a+2)` is **`3^a`**, which divides this difference.
Therefore `10^L'=10^L mod3^(a+2)`, retaining sufficient precision before
division by nine. The order modulo `17^b` is `16*17^(b-1)`, which also
divides the difference. The chosen e is unchanged. The higher r' therefore
satisfies every lower congruence, and CRT uniqueness gives `r'=r mod W`.

For `x_(m,j)=r_(m,j)/W_(m,j)` on the circle `T=R/Z`, the correct identities are

```
U*x_(m+1,j)=x_(m,j),
V*x_(m,j+1)=x_(m,j)       in T.
```

Multiplication takes the higher-index point to the lower-index point.
Put `K_N=closure{x_(m,j):m,j>=N}` and `E=intersection_N K_N`. Each K_N
is nonempty compact, and the K_N are nested. Thus E is nonempty, compact,
and closed.

Membership in E is equivalent to being the limit of a sequence of grid
points with **both** indices tending to infinity: for the forward direction
choose `m_q,j_q>=q` within circle distance `1/q` of the point. The reverse
direction follows from closedness of each K_N.

If `x_(m_q,j_q)->y` with both indices tending to infinity, then
`U*x_(m_q,j_q)=x_(m_q-1,j_q)` eventually. Both shifted indices still tend
to infinity, giving `Uy in E`. To obtain a preimage of y, take a convergent
subsequence of `x_(m_q+1,j_q)`; its limit z lies in E and satisfies `Uz=y`.
The same down/up shifts in j prove `V(E)=E`. Hence `U(E)=E=V(E)`, including
the surjectivity needed below. No single-row limit replaces a joint limit.

## 4. Why E cannot be finite

Assume it is finite. Surjectivity makes multiplication by U a permutation
of E. Take a common multiple h of its cycle lengths and `D=U^h-1>0`.
Then `Dy=0 in T` for every y in E; D is one common denominator.

Choose `0<epsilon<min(1/2,1/(U+1))`. There is N such that
`||D*x_(m,j)||<epsilon` for **every** `m,j>=N`. Otherwise choose violating
points with `m_q,j_q>=q`. Compactness supplies a subsequential limit in E,
and continuity gives its distance from zero after multiplication by D at
least epsilon, a contradiction. This establishes uniform proximity on a
whole quadrant, not just on a sequence.

It is now legitimate to fix `j=N`. For every `m>=N`, write the unique signed
representative as `z_m=D*r_(m,N)/W_(m,N)-q_m` in `(-epsilon,epsilon)`,
with integer q_m. The shift identity makes `U*z_(m+1)-z_m` an integer,
of absolute value strictly less than `(U+1)*epsilon<1`. Thus

```
z_m=U*z_(m+1),
W_(m+1,N)*z_(m+1)=W_(m,N)*z_m=C.
```

Furthermore `C=D*r_(m,N)-q_m*W_(m,N)` is an integer. It may be negative
or zero. The same D,C now give `D*r_(m,N)=C mod3^(a-2)` for every m on
this row, while `a=12+Am` tends to infinity. No uniformity in a moving row
is needed.

Set `X_m=10^L` at these row points. The exponent order gives
`10^(D*r)=10^C mod3^a`, interpreting negative powers through the inverse
of the unit 10. From `3^(a+2) | F`, reduction modulo `3^a` gives
`7X_m-25=18*10^r`. Also `7L=S+4` and `3^(a-2) | S` give
`X_m^7=10^(S+4)=10^4 mod3^a`.

Write `Cplus=max(C,0)`, `Cminus=max(-C,0)`. The two **fixed** integer
polynomials

```
P(X)=X^7-10^4,
Q(X)=10^Cminus*(7X-25)^D-18^D*10^Cplus
```

vanish at X_m modulo unbounded powers `3^a`. For C<0 the factor
`10^Cminus` clears the inverse; for C=0 this is simply
`Q=(7X-25)^D-18^D`. No division by 18 or by a power of three occurs.

If P,Q were coprime in `Q[X]`, clearing denominators in a polynomial
Bezout identity would give `A(X)P(X)+B(X)Q(X)=J`, with `A,B in Z[X]`
and a fixed nonzero integer J. Substituting the varying X_m would make
`3^a | J` for arbitrarily large a. This is impossible. The modular roots
need not coincide, lift compatibly, or converge for this argument.
Therefore P,Q share a complex root.

To check irreducibility, let `t=10^(4/7)>0`. The field `Q(t)` contains
`t^2/10=10^(1/7)`, of degree seven by Eisenstein at 2 for `X^7-10`.
Since P has degree seven and vanishes at t, t has degree exactly seven,
and P is irreducible. A common root therefore forces `P | Q` over Q.
For a primitive seventh root of unity zeta, both t and `t*zeta` would
then satisfy

```
(7x-25)^D=18^D*10^C.
```

But direct expansion gives

```
|7t*zeta-25|^2-|7t-25|^2=350*t*(1-Re(zeta))>0.
```

As D>0, the absolute values of their Dth powers cannot be equal. This
contradiction works for every integer C, proving E infinite.

## 5. The precise topological theorem and actual positions

The published closed-set form needed here appears in Bourgain,
Lindenstrauss, Michel and Venkatesh, *Some effective results for ×a ×b*,
**Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems 29** (2009), 1705–1722, **§1.1,
printed p.1705**: for multiplicatively independent integers a,b>1, a
closed infinite subset of the circle preserved by multiplication by
both integers must be the whole circle. This is a set theorem, with no
measure, positive-entropy, or equidistribution hypothesis.
[Published PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/01225FAD40EEBC38F3AE1A5C119D0267/S0143385708000898a.pdf/some-effective-results-for-ab.pdf).

E is closed and infinite and satisfies `U(E)=V(E)=E`. Moreover
`U=3^A>1`, `V=17^B>1` are multiplicatively independent by unique prime
factorization. All hypotheses hold, so `E=T`. In particular every K_N
is the full circle: every joint tail, not merely the union of the grid,
is dense.

Furstenberg's original source is *Disjointness in ergodic theory, minimal
sets, and a problem in diophantine approximation*, **Mathematical Systems
Theory 1** (1967), 1–49, Part IV, DOI
[10.1007/BF01692494](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01692494).
Its Theorem IV.1, printed p.48, states the irrational-orbit form for a
non-lacunary semigroup; the directly applicable closed-set statement
above avoids needing to prove that E contains an irrational first.
[Original paper scan](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~asalehig/F_Disjointness.pdf).

Compute exactly

```
W/L=(112*7^k/153)*S/(S+4) -> lambda_k=112*7^k/153.
```

Because k is fixed, lambda_k is a finite positive constant. In fact
`lambda_k>=784/153>1`, so for every `t in I`, `y=t/lambda_k` is strictly
between 0 and 1. The submission's `h=floor(t/lambda_k)` is always zero;
this is an optional simplification, not a repair.

Choose eta>0 with `0<t-eta<t+eta<1` and `(t-eta,t+eta)` inside I, and choose an arc
around y contained in the ordinary real interval `(0,1)`. Joint-tail
density lets us choose `m_q,j_q>=q` with the least-representative fractions
`x_(m_q,j_q)->y` in real coordinates, with no wrap at zero. Then
`S,L->infinity` and

```
r/L=x*(W/L) -> t.
```

More explicitly, if `|x-y|<delta`,

```
|r/L-t| <= lambda_k*delta + 4t/(S+4).
```

Choose `delta<eta/(2lambda_k)` (also keeping the arc inside `(0,1)`),
then S large enough for the second term to be less than eta/2. This
puts r/L strictly in I. Since `r/L>t-eta>0` and L grows, eventually r>=3;
since `r/L<t+eta<1`, r<L. The lengths are unbounded, yielding infinitely
many distinct lengths. Alternatively choose both indices beyond their
previous values at each step to make lengths strictly increase.

This covers intervals arbitrarily close to either endpoint: each fixed
nonempty open interval still contains a t with positive margin. For
`I=(41/100,42/100)`, choose `t=83/200` and any `eta<1/200`. For these actual
positions the three congruences, together with the coprime factor 25,
give `225S | F` and `v_7(F)=k`, completing the proof.

## What this establishes and what remains open

The theorem settles the arbitrary fixed-depth existence question left
open in PR #12, with exact depth and every nonempty open ratio interval.
It also rules out a uniform constant bound on depths of full-sum
survivors in any such interval.

It does not give a full witness or exclude all lengths of the `{5,7}`
subclass. The order of quantifiers is fixed k first, then arbitrarily
large L. Both the grid and the density neighbourhood depend on k;
`lambda_k` grows exponentially. Substituting `k=L-2` is not justified.
For sufficiently large constructed L, exact k is strictly below L-2,
so those particular pairs fail the full product condition. This does not
exclude other positions at their lengths.

The remaining global obligation is to reject the full condition
`225S*7^(L-2) | F` uniformly for all remaining admissible lengths and
positions, or exhibit a pair that satisfies it. The inherited
qualitative sublinear valuation bound and earlier finite coverage are
consistent with arbitrary fixed-depth existence.

The original [OEIS A398769 entry](https://oeis.org/A398769/internal)
defines odd integers divisible by the product of their digit product
and digit sum and conjectures that every term contains digit 1. This
audit neither proves nor disproves that broader conjecture.
